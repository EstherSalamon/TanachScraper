**2 [Hebrew]**	**וּמִקְצֵה אֶחָיו.** מִן הַפְּחוּתִים שֶׁבָּהֶם לִגְבוּרָה, שֶׁאֵין נִרְאִים גִּבּוֹרִים, שֶׁאִם יִרְאֶה אוֹתָם גִּבּוֹרִים יַעֲשֶׂה אוֹתָם אַנְשֵׁי מִלְחַמְתּוֹ, וְאֵלֶּה הֵם: רְאוּבֵן, שִׁמְעוֹן, לֵוִי, יִשָּׂשכָר וּבִנְיָמִין – אוֹתָן שֶׁלֹּא כָּפַל מֹשֶׁה שְׁמוֹתָם כְּשֶׁבֵּרְכָן; אֲבָל שְׁמוֹת הַגִּבּוֹרִים כָּפַל וְזֹאת לִיהוּדָה שְׁמַע ה' קוֹל יְהוּדָה; וּלְגָד אָמַר בָּרוּךְ מַרְחִיב גָּד; וּלְנַפְתָּלִי אָמַר נַפְתָּלִי, וּלְדָן אָמַר דָּן, וְכֵן לִזְבוּלוּן, וְכֵן לְאָשֵׁר; זֶהוּ לְשׁוֹן בּרֵאשִׁית רַבָּה, שֶׁהִיא אַגָּדַת אֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל. אֲבָל בְּתַלְמוּד בַּבְלִית שֶׁלָּנוּ (בבא קמא צ"ב) מָצִינוּ, שֶׁאוֹתָן שֶׁכָּפַל מֹשֶׁה שְׁמוֹתָן הֵם הַחַלָּשִׁים, וְאוֹתָן הֵבִיא לִפְנֵי פַרְעֹה, וִיהוּדָה שֶׁהֻכְפַּל שְׁמוֹ לֹא הֻכְפַּל מִשּׁוּם חַלָּשׁוּת, אֶלָּא טַעַם יֵשׁ בַּדָּבָר, כִּדְאִיתָא בְּבָּבָא קַמָּא וּבְבָרַיְתָא דְּסִפְרֵי שָׁנִינוּ בָהּ בִּוְזֹאת הַבְּרָכָה, כְּמוֹ תַּלְמוּד שֶׁלָּנוּ:<br>
**2 [English]**	**And from among his brothers.** From the most inferior of them in regards to physical strength, [i.e., those] who did not appear strong, for if he [Pharaoh] recognized them as being strong, he would make them his warriors. They are the following: Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Issachar, and Benjamin, those whose names Moses did not double when he blessed them (Deut. 33), but the names of the strong ones he doubled, [as follows:] “And this is for Judah…Hear, O Lord, the voice of Judah” (Deut. 33:7). “And regarding Gad he said, ‘Blessed be He Who granted space to Gad’ ” (ibid. 20). “And regarding Naphtali he said, ‘Naphtali’s wishes shall be well satisfied’ ” (ibid. 23). “And regarding Dan, he said, ‘Dan is a young lion’ ” (ibid. 22). And so on for Zebulun (ibid. 18), and for Asher (ibid. 24). This is a quotation from Genesis Rabbah (95:4), which is the Aggadah of Eretz Israel. In our Babylonian Talmud, however, we find that those whose names Moses doubled were the weak ones, and it was they whom he brought before Pharaoh. As for Judah (the sixth one), whose name was doubled, however, it was not doubled because of weakness, but there is a[nother] reason for it, as is stated in Baba Kamma (92a). In the Baraitha of Sifrei, in “Vezoth Haberachah” (354) we learn as in our Talmud. [I.e., the Sifrei identifies the five brothers as does the Talmud, namely that the five brothers were Gad, Naphtali, Dan, Zebulun, and Asher.]



**6 [Hebrew]**	**אַנְשֵׁי־חַיִל.** בְּקִיאִין בְּאֻמָּנוּתָן לִרְעוֹת צֹאן:<br>
**6 [English]**	**capable men.** Skillful in their occupation of pasturing sheep.

**6 [Hebrew]**	**עַל־אֲשֶׁר־לִֽי.** עַל צֹאן שֶׁלִּי:<br>
**6 [English]**	**over what is mine.** Over my flocks.



**7 [Hebrew]**	**וַיְבָרֶךְ יַֽעֲקֹב.** הִיא שְׁאִילַת שָׁלוֹם כְּדֶרֶךְ כָּל הַנִּרְאִים לִפְנֵי הַמְּלָכִים לִפְרָקִים, שלו"איר בְּלַעַז:<br>
**7 [English]**	**and Jacob greeted.** Heb. וַיְבָרֶךְ. This is a greeting, as is customary for all who occasionally present themselves before monarchs, saluder in Old French. [From Tanchuma Nasso 26]



**9 [Hebrew]**	**שְׁנֵי מְגוּרַי.** יְמֵי גֵרוּתִי; כָּל יָמַי הָיִיתִי גֵּר בָּאָרֶץ:<br>
**9 [English]**	**the years of my sojournings.** The days of my being a stranger. All my days, I have been a stranger in the land.

**9 [Hebrew]**	**וְלֹא הִשִּׂיגוּ.** בְּטוֹבָה:<br>
**9 [English]**	**and they have not reached.** in goodness.



**10 [Hebrew]**	**וַיְבָרֶךְ יַֽעֲקֹב.** כְּדֶרֶךְ כָּל הַנִּפְטָרִים מִלִּפְנֵי שָׂרִים מְבָרְכִים אוֹתָם וְנוֹטְלִים רְשׁוּת, וּמַה בְּרָכָה בֵּרְכוֹ? שֶׁיַּעֲלֶה נִילוּס לְרַגְלָיו, לְפִי שֶׁאֵין מִצְרַיִם שׁוֹתָה מֵי גְשָׁמִים אֶלָּא נִילוּס עוֹלֶה וּמַשְׁקֶה, וּמִבִּרְכָתוֹ שֶׁל יַעֲקֹב וָאֵילָךְ הָיָה פַרְעֹה בָא אֶל נִילוּס וְהוּא עוֹלֶה לִקְרָאתוֹ וּמַשְׁקֶה אֶת הָאָרֶץ (תַּנְחוּמָא):<br>
**10 [English]**	**So Jacob blessed.** According to the custom of all those who leave the presence of princes, that they bless them and take their leave. Now what blessing did he bless him? That the Nile should rise at his approach, because Egypt does not drink rain water, but the Nile rises and waters it, and since Jacob’s blessing, Pharaoh would come to the Nile, and it would rise to greet him and water the land. [From Tanchuma, Nasso 26]



**11 [Hebrew]**	**רַעְמְסֵס.** מֵאֶרֶץ גֹּשֶׁן הִיא:<br>
**11 [English]**	**Rameses.** That is [part] of the land of Goshen.



**12 [Hebrew]**	**לְפִי הַטָּֽף.** לְפִי הַצָּרִיךְ לְכָל בְּנֵי בֵיתָם:<br>
**12 [English]**	**[with] bread according to the young children.** According to what was required for all the members of their household.



**13 [Hebrew]**	**וְלֶחֶם אֵין בְּכָל־הָאָרֶץ.** חוֹזֵר לָעִנְיָן הָרִאשׁוֹן – לִתְחִלַּת שְׁנֵי הָרָעָב:<br>
**13 [English]**	**Now there was no food in the entire land.** It (Scripture) returns to the earlier topic, to the beginning of the famine years. [From Gen. Rabbah 89:9]

**13 [Hebrew]**	**וַתֵּלַהּ.** כְּמוֹ וַתִּלְאֶה, לְשׁוֹן עֲיֵפוּת, כְּתַרְגּוּמוֹ; וְדוֹמֶה לוֹ כְּמִתְלַהְלֵהַּ הַיֹּרֶה זִקִּים (משלי כ"ו):<br>
**13 [English]**	**were exhausted.** An expression of exhaustion, as the Targum renders ואשתלהי, and similar to this is “Like one who wearies himself shooting firebrands” (Prov. 26:18).



**14 [Hebrew]**	**בַּשֶּׁבֶר אֲשֶׁר־הֵם שֹֽׁבְרִים.** נוֹתְנִין לוֹ אֶת הַכֶּסֶף:<br>
**14 [English]**	**with the grain that they were buying.** they gave him the money.



**15 [Hebrew]**	**אָפֵס.** כְּתַרְגּוּמוֹ שְׁלִים:<br>
**15 [English]**	**has been used up.** Heb. אָפֵס, as the Targum renders: שְׁלִים, [meaning] is ended. — [Rashi]



**17 [Hebrew]**	**וַיְנַֽהֲלֵם.** כְּמוֹ וַיְנַהֲגֵם, וְדוֹמֶה לוֹ אֵין מְנַהֵל לָהּ (ישעיהו נ"א), עַל מֵי מְנֻחוֹת יְנַהֲלֵנִי (תהילים כ"ג):<br>
**17 [English]**	**and he provided them.** Heb. וַיְנַהִלֵם, similar to וַיְנַהִגֵם, and he guided them, and similar to this is “She has no guide (מְנַהֵל)” (Isa. 51:18),“ He leads me (יְנַהִלֵנִי) beside still waters” (Ps. 23:2).



**18 [Hebrew]**	**בַּשָּׁנָה הַשֵּׁנִית.** לִשְׁנֵי הָרָעָב:<br>
**18 [English]**	**in the second year.** The second of the famine years.

**18 [Hebrew]**	**כִּי אִם־תַּם הַכֶּסֶף וגו'.** כִּי אֲשֶׁר תַּם הַכֶּסֶף וְהַמִּקְנֶה וּבָא הַכֹּל אֶל יַד אֲדוֹנִי:<br>
**18 [English]**	**for insofar as the money and the property in animals have been forfeited, etc.** Heb. כִּי אִם. For insofar as the money and the property have been forfeited and everything has come into my lord’s possession.

**18 [Hebrew]**	**בִּלְתִּי אִם־גְּוִיָּתֵנוּ.** כְּמוֹ אִם לֹא גְּוִיָּתֵנוּ:<br>
**18 [English]**	**except our bodies.** Heb. בִּלְתִּי, like אִם לֹא גְוִיָתֵנוּ, if not our bodies.



**19 [Hebrew]**	**וְתֶן־זֶרַע.** לִזְרֹעַ הָאֲדָמָה; וְאַף עַל פִּי שֶׁאָמַר יוֹסֵף וְעוֹד חָמֵשׁ שָׁנִים אֲשֶׁר אֵין חָרִישׁ וְקָצִיר, מִכֵּיוָן שֶׁבָּא יַעֲקֹב לְמִצְרַיִם בָּאָה בְרָכָה לְרַגְלָיו וְהִתְחִיל לִזְרֹעַ, וְכָלָה הָרָעָב, וְכֵן שָׁנִינוּ בְּתוֹסֶפְתָּא דְּסוֹטָה:<br>
**19 [English]**	**and give [us] seed-.** [with which] to sow the soil. Although Joseph said, “and [for] another five years there will be neither plowing nor harvest” (Gen. 45:6), as soon as Jacob came to Egypt, blessing came with his arrival, and they started to sow, and the famine ended. So we learned in the Tosefta of Sotah (10:1-3).

**19 [Hebrew]**	**לֹא תֵשָֽׁם.** לֹא תְהֵא שְׁמָמָה; לָא תְבוּר – לְשׁוֹן שְׂדֵה בּוּר, שֶׁאֵינוֹ חָרוּשׁ:<br>
**19 [English]**	**will not lie fallow.** Heb. לֹא תֵּשָׁם, will not be desolate, [and Onkelos renders:] לָא תְבוּר, an expression denoting a fallow field (שָׂדֶה בוּר), which is not plowed.



**20 [Hebrew]**	**וַתְּהִי הָאָרֶץ לְפַרְעֹה.** קְנוּיָה לוֹ:<br>
**20 [English]**	**and the land became Pharaoh’s.** I.e., it was acquired by him.



**21 [Hebrew]**	**וְאֶת־הָעָם הֶֽעֱבִיר.** יוֹסֵף מֵעִיר לְעִיר לְזִכָּרוֹן, שֶׁאֵין לָהֶם עוֹד חֵלֶק בָּאָרֶץ, וְהוֹשִׁיב שֶׁל עִיר זוֹ בַחֲבֶרְתָּהּ, וְלֹא הֻצְרַךְ הַכָּתוּב לִכְתֹּב זֹאת אֶלָּא לְהוֹדִיעֲךָ שִׁבְחוֹ שֶׁל יוֹסֵף שֶׁנִּתְכַּוֵּן לְהָסִיר חֶרְפָּה מֵעַל אֶחָיו, שֶׁלֹא יִהְיוּ קוֹרִין אוֹתָם גּוֹלִים (חולין ס'):<br>
**21 [English]**	**And he transferred the populace.** Joseph [transferred them] from city to city so that they would remember that they have no more share in the land, and he settled those of one city in another (Targum Onkelos). Scripture did not have to write this except to let you know Joseph’s praise, that he intended to remove the stigma from his brothers, so that they (the Egyptians) would not call them exiles. [From Gen. Rabbah 89:9, Chul. 60b]

**21 [Hebrew]**	**מִקְצֵה גְבֽוּל־מִצְרַיִם וגו'.** כֵּן עָשָׂה לְכָל הֶעָרִים אֲשֶׁר בְּמַלְכוּת מִצְרַיִם, מִקְצֵה גְבוּלָהּ וְעַד קְצֵה גְבוּלָהּ:<br>
**21 [English]**	**from [one] end of the boundary of Egypt, etc..** So he did with all the cities in the kingdom of Egypt, from one end of its boundary to the other end of its boundary.



**22 [Hebrew]**	**הַכֹּֽהֲנִים.** הַכּוּמָרִים, כָּל לְשׁוֹן כֹּהֵן מְשָׁרֵת לֶאֱלֹהוּת הוּא, חוּץ מֵאוֹתָן שֶׁהֵם לְשׁוֹן גְּדֻלָּה, כְּמוֹ כֹּהֵן מִדְיָן, כֹּהֵן אוֹן:<br>
**22 [English]**	**the priests.** Heb. הַכֹּהִנִים, the priests. Every instance of כֹּהֵן means a minister to deities, except those that are an expression of high rank, like “the governor (כֹּהֵן) of Midian” (Exod. 2:16), “the governor (כֹּהֵן) of On” (Gen. 41:45). [From targumim]

**22 [Hebrew]**	**חֹק לַכֹּֽהֲנִים.** חֹק כָּךְ וְכָךְ לֶחֶם לַיּוֹם:<br>
**22 [English]**	**the priests had an allotment.** An allotment of so much bread per day.



**23 [Hebrew]**	**הֵֽא־.** כְּמוֹ הִנֵּה, כְּמוֹ וְגַם אֲנִי הֵא דַּרְכֵּךְ בְּרֹאשׁ נָתַתִּי (יחזקאל ט"ז):<br>
**23 [English]**	**Behold.** Heb. הֵא, equivalent to הִנֵה, as in: “behold (הֵא) I have laid your way on [your] head” (Ezek. 16:43). [From targumim]



**24 [Hebrew]**	**לְזֶרַע הַשָּׂדֶה.** שֶׁבְּכָל שָׁנָה:<br>
**24 [English]**	**for seed for [your] field[s].** Every year.

**24 [Hebrew]**	**וְלַֽאֲשֶׁר בְּבָֽתֵּיכֶם.** וְלֶאֱכֹל הָעֲבָדִים וְהַשְּׁפָחוֹת אֲשֶׁר בְּבָתֵּיכֶם:<br>
**24 [English]**	**for those in your houses.** For food for the manservants and maidservants who are in your houses.

**24 [Hebrew]**	**לְטַפְּכֶֽם.** בָּנִים קְטַנִּים:<br>
**24 [English]**	**your young children.** Heb. טַפְּכֶם, young children.



**25 [Hebrew]**	**נִמְצָא־חֵן.** לַעֲשׂוֹת לָנוּ זֹאת כְּמוֹ שֶׁאָמַרְתָּ:<br>
**25 [English]**	**Let us find favor.** that you should do this for us, as you have said.

**25 [Hebrew]**	**וְהָיִינוּ עֲבָדִים לְפַרְעֹֽה.** לְהַעֲלוֹת לוֹ הַמַּס הַזֶּה בְּכָל שָׁנָה:<br>
**25 [English]**	**and we will be slaves to Pharaoh.** to pay him this tribute every year as a statute that will not be repealed.



**27 [Hebrew]**	**וַיֵּשֶׁב יִשְׂרָאֵל בְּאֶרֶץ מִצְרַיִם.** וְהֵיכָן? בְּאֶרֶץ גֹּשֶׁן שֶׁהִיא מֵאֶרֶץ מִצְרַיִם:<br>
**27 [English]**	**And Israel dwelt in the land of Egypt.** Where? In the land of Goshen, which is [part] of the land of Egypt.

**27 [Hebrew]**	**וַיֵּאָֽחֲזוּ בָהּ.** לְשׁוֹן אֲחֻזָּה):<br>
**27 [English]**	**and they acquired property in it.** Heb. וַיֵאָחִזוּ בָהּ, [which is] an expression of אִחֻזָה, holding.



**28 [Hebrew]**	**וַיְחִי יַֽעֲקֹב.** לָמָּה פָּרָשָׁה זוֹ סְתוּמָה? לְפִי שֶׁכֵּיוָן שֶׁנִּפְטַר יַעֲקֹב אָבִינוּ נִסְתְּמוּ עֵינֵיהֶם וְלִבָּם שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל מִצָּרַת הַשִּׁעְבּוּד, שֶׁהִתְחִילוּ לְשַׁעְבְּדָם; דָּבָר אַחֵר: שֶׁבִּקֵּשׁ לְגַלּוֹת אֶת הַקֵּץ לְבָנָיו, וְנִסְתַּם מִמֶּנּוּ. בּבְּרֵאשִׁית רַבָּה:<br>
**28 [English]**	**And Jacob lived.** Why is this section [completely] closed? Because, as soon as our father Jacob passed away, the eyes and the heart of Israel were “closed,” (i.e., it became “dark” for them) because of the misery of the slavery, for they (the Egyptians) commenced to subjugate them. Another explanation: That he (Jacob) attempted to reveal the End [of the exile] to his sons, but it was “closed off” (concealed) from him. [This appears] in Gen. Rabbah (91:1).



**29 [Hebrew]**	**וַיִּקְרְבוּ יְמֵֽי־יִשְׂרָאֵל לָמוּת.** כָּל מִי שֶׁנֶּאֱמְרָה בּוֹ קְרִיבָה לָמוּת, לֹא הִגִּיעַ לִימֵי אֲבוֹתָיו (יִצְחָק חָיָה ק"פ, וְיַעֲקֹב קמ"ז, בְּדָוִד נֶאֱמַר קְרִיבָה, אָבִיו חָיָה פ' שָׁנִים וְהוּא חָיָה ע'):<br>
**29 [English]**	**When the time drew near for Israel to die.** Everyone of whom it is stated [that his days] drew near to die, did not attain the life span of his forefathers. [Isaac lived 180 years, and Jacob lived only 147 years. In connection with David, the expression of drawing near is mentioned (I Kings 2:1). His father lived 80 years, and he lived 70.]- [from Gen. Rabbah 96:4]

**29 [Hebrew]**	**וַיִּקְרָא לִבְנוֹ לְיוֹסֵף.** לְמִי שֶׁהָיָה יְכֹלֶת בְּיָדוֹ לַעֲשׂוֹת:<br>
**29 [English]**	**he called his son Joseph.** The one who had the ability to do it. [from Gen. Rabbah 96:5]

**29 [Hebrew]**	**שִֽׂים־נָא יָֽדְךָ.** וְהִשָּׁבַע:<br>
**29 [English]**	**now place your hand beneath my thigh.** And swear. — [from Pirkei d’Rabbi Eliezer ch. 39] As explained in the narrative of Abraham and Eliezer (Gen. 24:2), he meant that Joseph should swear by covenant of the circumcision.

**29 [Hebrew]**	**חֶסֶד וֶֽאֱמֶת.** חֶסֶד שֶׁעוֹשִׂין עִם הַמֵּתִים הוּא חֶסֶד שֶׁל אֱמֶת, שֶׁאֵינוֹ מְצַפֶּה לְתַשְׁלוּם גְּמוּל:<br>
**29 [English]**	**lovingkindness and truth.** Lovingkindness that is done with the dead is true lovingkindness, for one does not expect any payment or reward. — [from Gen. Rabbah 96:5]

**29 [Hebrew]**	**אַל־נָא תִקְבְּרֵנִי בְּמִצְרָֽיִם.** סוֹפָהּ לִהְיוֹת עֲפָרָהּ כִּנִּים, וּמְרַחֲשִׁין תַּחַת גּוּפִי, וְשֶׁאֵין מֵתֵי חוּצָה לָאָרֶץ חַיִּים אֶלָּא בְּצַעַר גִּלְגּוּל מְחִלּוֹת, וְשֶׁלֹּא יַעֲשׂוּנִי מִצְרַיִם עֲבוֹדָה זָרָה:<br>
**29 [English]**	**do not bury me now in Egypt.** [Because] its soil is destined to become lice (which will crawl under my body), and because those who die outside the [Holy] Land will not be resurrected except with the pain of rolling through underground passages. [Also] so that the Egyptians will not deify me. — [from Gen. Rabbah 96:5, Keth. 111a]



**30 [Hebrew]**	**וְשָֽׁכַבְתִּי עִם־אבותי.** וָי"ו זוֹ מְחֻבָּר לְמַעְלָה לִתְחִלַּת הַמִּקְרָא – שִׂים נָא יָדְךָ תַּחַת יְרֵכִי וְהִשָּׁבַע לִי – וַאֲנִי סוֹפִי לִשְׁכַּב עִם אֲבוֹתַי, וְאַתָּה תִּשָּׂאֵנִי מִמִּצְרַיִם. וְאֵין לוֹמַר, וְשָׁכַבְתִּי עִם אֲבוֹתַי – הַשְׁכִּיבֵנִי עִם אֲבוֹתַי בַּמְּעָרָה, שֶׁהֲרֵי כְּתִיב אַחֲרָיו, וּנְשָׂאתַנִי מִמִּצְרַיִם וּקְבַרְתַּנִי בִּקְבֻרָתָם; וְעוֹד, מָצִינוּ בְּכָל מָקוֹם לְשׁוֹן שְׁכִיבָה עִם אֲבוֹתָיו הִיא הַגְּוִיעָה וְלֹא הַקְּבוּרָה, כְּמוֹ: וַיִּשְׁכַּב דָּוִד עִם אֲבֹתָיו, וְאַחַר כָּךְ וַיִּקָּבֵר בְּעִיר דָּוִד (מלכים א ב'):<br>
**30 [English]**	**I will lie with my forefathers.** Heb. וְשָׁכַבְתִּי, lit., and I will lie. This “vav” (of וְשָׁכַבְתִּי) is connected to the beginning of the previous verse: “Place now your hand beneath my thigh and swear to me, for I am destined to lie with my forefathers, and you shall carry me out of Egypt.” We cannot say, however, that “I will lie with my forefathers” means: Lay me to rest with my forefathers in the cave, because afterwards it is written: “and you shall carry me out of Egypt, and you shall bury me in their grave.” Moreover, we find everywhere that the expression “lying with one’s forefathers” denotes expiration, not burial, as in “And David lay with his forefathers,” and afterwards, “and he was buried in the city of David” (I Kings 2:10).



**31 [Hebrew]**	**וַיִּשְׁתַּחוּ יִשְׂרָאֵל.** תַּעֲלָא בְּעִידָּנֵיהּ סְגִיד לֵיהּ:<br>
**31 [English]**	**and Israel prostrated himself.** [Although the lion is king] when it is the time of the fox, bow down to him. — [from Meg. 16b]

**31 [Hebrew]**	**עַל־רֹאשׁ הַמִּטָּֽה.** הָפַךְ עַצְמוֹ לְצַד הַשְּׁכִינָה. מִכָּאן אָמְרוּ שֶׁהַשְּׁכִינָה לְמַעְלָה מֵרַאֲשׁוֹתָיו שֶׁל חוֹלֶה (שבת י"ב, נדרים מ'); דָּבָר אַחֵר עַל רֹאשׁ הַמִּטָּה – עַל שֶׁהָיְתָה מִטָּתוֹ שְׁלֵמָה וְלֹא הָיָה בָהּ רֶשַׁע, שֶׁהֲרֵי יוֹסֵף מֶלֶךְ הוּא, וְעוֹד שֶׁנִּשְׁבָּה לְבֵין הַגּוֹיִם, וַהֲרֵי הוּא עוֹמֵד בְּצִדְקוֹ:<br>
**31 [English]**	**on the head of the bed.** He turned around to the side of the Shechinah (Gen. Rabbah, Vatican ms. no. 60). From here [the Sages] deduced that the Shechinah is at the head of a sick person (Shab. 12b). Another explanation: עַל רֹאשׁ הַמִטָה - [He prostrated himself to God] because his offspring were perfect, insofar as not one of them was wicked, as is evidenced by the fact that Joseph was a king, and furthermore, that [even though] he was captured among the heathens, he remained steadfast in his righteousness. — [from Sifrei Va’ethannan 31, Sifrei Ha’azinu 334]
