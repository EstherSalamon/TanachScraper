**2**	**How was your mother a lioness!.** How esteemed was your mother, you, Zedekiah that she was compared to a lioness!

**2**	**Among the lions.** The princes of Judah.



**3**	**And she raised up one of her cubs.** This lamentation was said regarding the sons of Josiah. The first is Jehoahaz.

**3**	**he devoured men.** Heb. אָדָם. He robbed Israel, for Israel is called אָדָם, man, as is stated (below 34:31): “And you are My flock, the flock of My pasture; you are men,” and according to the allegory, any beast that tasted human flesh will always provoke humans.



**4**	**Nations gathered around him.** Pharaoh the king of Egypt.

**4**	**in their pit he was caught.** Heb. בְּשַּׁחְתָם. They dig pits and cover their openings with straw, and the beast falls into them.

**4**	**and they brought him with hooks.** A sort of iron ring. They puncture his nose and pull him with them.



**5**	**Now she saw.** His mother, the congregation of Israel.

**5**	**that she had taken ill.** Heb. נוֹחַלָה, an expression of illness (חֹלִי).

**5**	**and she took one of her cubs.** Jehoiakim.

**5**	**and made him a young lion.** [i.e.,] a king. Every כְּפִיר in Scripture is a young and mighty lion.



**7**	**And he was intimate with their widows.** He would rape the women and kill their husbands. Some interpret אַלְמְנוֹתָיו like אַרְמְנוֹתָיו [their palaces] and וַיֵדַע as an expression of destruction and ruin, as in (Ps. 138:6): “and He chastises (יְיֵדָע) the haughty from afar.”

**7**	**and their cities he laid waste.** For he levied a tax on the land to give Pharaoh annually, as it is said in II Kings (23:35).



**8**	**And nations gathered around against him from provinces.** וַיִתְּנוּ [is rendered by Jonathan as:] and they gathered.

**8**	**And nations gathered.** וַיִתְּנוּ [is rendered by Jonathan as:] and they gathered.



**9**	**into a collar.** Heb. בַּסוּגַר. Jonathan rendered: into a collar.

**9**	**into strongholds.** to be imprisoned there.



**10**	**Your mother is like a vine.** This is still part of the lamentation.

**10**	**in your likeness.** Heb. בְּדָמְךָ, a term denoting a likeness and an analogy; i.e., you are like one whose mother was like a vine planted by water.

**10**	**fruitful.** Heb. פֹּרִיָה, like (Deut. 29:17): “that bears (פּוֹרָה) gall,” [a word meaning:] produces fruit.

**10**	**from many waters.** Through many waters.



**11**	**sturdy rods for the scepters of the rulers.** Powerful rulers and mighty kings.

**11**	**over between the interwoven branches.** The trees whose branches were many.



**12**	**And it was scattered.** Heb. וַתֻּתֳּשּׁ. Every [expression of] נְתִישָּׁה or נְטִישָּׁה means something that lies scattered upon the face of the whole earth.

**12**	**and the east wind.** Nebuchadnezzar.

**12**	**dried up its fruit.** He slaughtered the sons of Zedekiah.



**14**	**And fire came out of the staff of its sturdy branches.** From the iniquity of its kings and princes, the evil came to it.

**14**	**It is a lamentation, and it will be a lamentation.** The prophecy is a lamentation, and it will be a lamentation.
