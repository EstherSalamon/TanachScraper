**2**	**propound a riddle.** The prophecy in this chapter is expressed as a riddle, [in] which he compares Nebuchadnezzar to an eagle and the kings of Judah to the lofty top of cedars.



**3**	**long pinions.** Heb. אֵבֶר like כָּנָף, wing, aile in French. Another explanation: as its apparent meaning, an expression of actual limbs.

**3**	**diverse colors.** Heb. קָרְמָה [lit. embroidered]. Its feathers are beautiful and ornate.

**3**	**the lofty top of the cedar.** Heb. צַמֶרֶת, all the foliage of the tree, cime in French. This is a parable representing Jehoiakim and his mighty men.



**4**	**He plucked the top of its branches.** Heb. יְנִיקוֹתָיו. He humbled his [Jehoiakim’s] greatness and his monarchy. יְנִיקוֹתָיו is like עַנָפָיו, its branches.

**4**	**and brought to a land of merchants.** Heb. כְּנַעַן, to a land of merchants; that is Babylon.



**5**	**Then he took of the seed of the land.** Zedekiah his son.

**5**	**in a fertile field.** [lit. in a field of seed.] In Jerusalem, in the capital city.

**5**	**it took [root] beside abundant waters.** Heb. קַח, it took. It became rooted beside abundant waters, i.e., he gave him greatness and dominion over his surroundings.

**5**	**a mountain willow he made it.** Heb. צַפְצָפָה, a species of willow tree which has many branches, saice in Old French, a willow.



**6**	**spreading.** Heb. סוֹרַחַת, like (Exod. 26:12): “And the overhanging length (וְסֶרַח),” large.

**6**	**of low stature.** to serve the king of Babylon.

**6**	**its tendrils.** Heb. דָּלִיוֹתָיו, its branches.

**6**	**to him.** to the king of Babylon.

**6**	**under him.** under the king of Babylon

**6**	**branches.** Heb. בַּדִים, staves.

**6**	**and sent forth.** It grew.

**6**	**boughs.** many leaves.



**7**	**Now there was one… eagle.** The king of Egypt.

**7**	**gathered its roots.** Heb. כָּפְנָה, an expression of hunger, like (Job 5:22): “plunder and famine (וּלְכָפָן).” The roots of this vine hungered and longed for that eagle. [Addendum: Some explain כָּפְנָה like אָסְפָה, gathered, the letters [of כָּפְנָה] being transposed. In the words of our Sages there is something similar to it, for they call a public gathering כְּנוּפְיָא. כָּפְנָה is like כָּנְפָה, amassa in French, brought in, gathered.]

**7**	**and its tendrils it sent forth to him.** And her emissaries she sent to him, to come to her and water her.

**7**	**from the furrows of its planting.** From the water ditches that the first eagle had made for it.



**8**	**In a good field, etc..** i.e., she did this for no purpose, because she had been planted in a good field.

**8**	**a sturdy vine.** Heb. אַדָרֶת, a sturdy vine.



**9**	**Say.** The entire Hebrew alphabet is found in this verse, and I am puzzled that it is not listed in the Masorah.

**9**	**[Will he not] pull up its roots.** The first eagle.

**9**	**and cut off its fruits.** Heb. יְקוֹסֵס, like יְקוֹצֵץ, will cut off. They are Zedekiah’s sons, whom he slaughtered before his eyes.

**9**	**the leaves of.** Heb. טַרְפֵי, leaves, feuilles in French.

**9**	**And not with great power and many people.** will the second eagle come to their assistance.

**9**	**against those who rip it out by its roots.** Against those who uproot and pull it out by its roots.



**10**	**And behold.** now it is planted as they wished.

**10**	**will it prosper.** Is it possible that it will prosper, this one that betrayed the one who planted it originally?



**12**	**what these are.** What this riddle is, and he explained it to them.

**12**	**took away its king and its princes.** They are the “lofty top of the cedar,” Jehoiakim and his princes.

**12**	**to Babylon.** That is the “city of traffickers.”



**13**	**of the royal seed.** Zedekiah.

**13**	**the mighty of the land.** The princes of the land.



**14**	**that it might stand.** That it might endure.



**17**	**when he cast up mounds.** When Nebuchadnezzar comes and casts up mounds against Jerusalem.

**17**	**to cut off many souls.** To slay the people of the city.



**18**	**he gave his hand.** He promised in good faith and raised his hand in a covenant.



**19**	**I will bring it upon his head.** [lit. put it in his head.] That is the blinding of his eyes.



**20**	**And I will spread My net over him.** as I explained above at the beginning of the Book (12:13): that He set up a deer to walk on the roof of the cave.

**20**	**and contend.** And I will debate with him.



**21**	**And all his fugitives.** Heb. מִבְרָחָיו. According to Targum, and all his mighty men. But Menachem (p. 48) associated it with (Song 8:10): “Flee (בְּרַח) my beloved,” and this is its interpretation: and all those who fled.

**21**	**with all his wings.** Heb. אַגַפָּיו with all their wings, i.e., with all their strength they will fall by the sword.

**21**	**will disperse.** Seront repandus, will be scattered.



**22**	**And I Myself will take.** And I will take the King Messiah.

**22**	**and I will place [it].** As the Targum renders: and I will establish him.

**22**	**a tender one.** It is customary to graft only from the tender [sprigs] that grew the previous year, called grayfe in Old French, a shoot, graft.

**22**	**and lofty.** Heb. וְתָלוּל, from the word mound, תֵּל, for a mound is usually high and lofty; amonceler in French, to heap up.



**23**	**shall they dwell.** Heb. תִּשְּׁכֹּנָה. The ‘dagesh’ in the ‘nun’ serves instead of a second ‘nun,’ as though it were written תִּשּׁכֹּנְנָה.



**24**	**I… have lowered the high tree.** The nations that were ruling over Israel.

**24**	**I have dried up the green tree.** Zedekiah and his seed.

**24**	**and have made the dry tree blossom.** Jehoiachin, who was exiled without children, will beget Zerubbabel in Babylon, and he will be the governor of Judea, [appointed] to rule.
