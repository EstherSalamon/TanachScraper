**1**	**By Gibeath moreh, in the valley.** (מִגִּבְעַת הַמּוֹרָה בָּעֵמֶק.) by the mountain that overlooks the plain (Targum Jonathan). הַמּוֹרָה is an expression of instructing and looking (וּרְאִיָּה הוֹרָאָה, as in (Prov. 6:13) “Instructing (מוֹרֶה) with his fingers,” because from there, one could see and signal into the valley.



**2**	**vaunt.** vanter in French (i.e., praise themselves)



**3**	**and depart early.** Heb. וְיִצְפֹּר. I.e., in the morning, as in the Aramaic term צַפְרָא (morning).



**5**	**“Every one who laps with his tongue, etc., and (likewise) every one that kneels upon his knees to drink.”.** Set him (i.e., he who kneels) separately away from your group because they will not go with you since they are thus accustomed to kneel before idols.



**6**	**of those that lapped, putting their hand….** This is not a kneeling procedure just as one who laps with his tongue (is not considered kneeling).



**12**	**נֹפְלִים בָּעֵמֶק.** I.e., they were encamped in the valley.



**13**	**צְלִיל.** This is written צְלִול (i.e., empty), because the generation was empty of righteous people. צְלִיל לֶחֶם שׂעוֹרִים A roasted barley bread (Targum Jonathan). A cake baked (צְלִי) on coals.

**13**	**barley bread.** Symbolizing the merit of the Omer sacrificed on Passover.

**13**	**and smote it, and it fell.** i.e., The tent.

**13**	**and it turned it.** i.e., The tent was completely overturned, and fell.



**15**	**telling of the dream.** Heb. מִסְפַּר). The story (סִפּוּר) of the dream, (as Targum Jonathan renders) יַת שוּעִית חֶלְמָא וְיַת פִּשְׁרֵיהּ (the telling of the dream and its interpretation).

**15**	**and its interpretation.** Its exchange; meaning, its interpretation (which takes the place of the dream).



**16**	**trumpets… torches.** To commemorate the merit of the giving of the Torah. However, the simple meaning is that since it was night, as it states (v. 9), it was dark, therefore they took the torches to provide light, and placed them into pitchers lest the torches be discerned.



**17**	**You shall look at me.** At what I do, and do likewise.



**18**	**I and all that are with me.** One of the companies consisting of one hundred men was with him.

**18**	**For the Lord and for Gideon.** (Targum Jonathan paraphrases:) The sword that kills is prompted by the Lord, and the victory is through Gideon.



**19**	**they had but recently awakened.** I.e., The soldiers previously (had but recently awakened) the guards, who were to guard during the middle watch; for so it is customary for soldiers to set up watchmen, these would watch for the first third of the night, these for the second third, and these for the last third.

**19**	**and they smashed the pitchers that were in their hands.** (and he told them) to break the pitchers which were in their hands.



**21**	**and they trumpeted.** A blast to disencamp and flee.



**24**	**and seize before them the waters.** Which separate between Edom and Canaan (Text per Rabbi Joseph Karo).

**24**	**and (also) the Jordan.** For it also separated.



**25**	**they brought to Gideon beyond the Jordan.** In the morning, when Gideon crossed the Jordan in pursuit of Zebah and Zalmunna.
