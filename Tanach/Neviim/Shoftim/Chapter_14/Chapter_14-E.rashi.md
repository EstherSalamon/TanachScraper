**4**	**that he sought a pretense.** I.e., a pretense to antagonize them.



**7**	**and he talked to the woman (lit.).** I.e., concerning the woman, he spoke to her relatives. So renders Targum Jonathan .



**8**	**עֲדַת דְּבוֹרִים.** (Targum Jonathan renders) דְּדַבַּרְיָתָא קִינָא, a swarm of bees.



**9**	**וַיִּרְדֵּהוּ.** This is an expression of separating anything which is clinging, as by bread clinging to an oven, its removal is called רְדִיָּה (Sab. 117b) “One who separates (הָרוֹדֶה) bread from an oven,” (ibid. 95a) “One who separates (הָרוֹדֶה) from his hive,” since the honey is contained in honeycombs clinging to the walls of the vessel on all sides from wall to wall according to the circumference of the vessel. Likewise Targum Jonathan renders וַיִּרְדֵּהוּ as וְנַסְחֵיהּ (and he separated).



**10**	**Now his father went down to the woman (lit.).** I.e., concerning the woman [after Targum Jonathan].



**11**	**מֵרֵעִים.** companions.



**15**	**And it was, on the seventh day.** Of the week, not the seventh (day) of the feasting but rather the fourth day of feasting.

**15**	**have you called us here to impoverish us.** Targum Jonathan renders: Have you called us here (הֲלֹא = הֲלוֹם) to impoverish us?



**17**	**the seven days.** I.e., the remainder of the seven days, from the fourth and onward.



**18**	**before the sun set.** Before sunset, since they still had time until evening.

**18**	**plowed with my heifer.** This is allegorical (as Targum Jonathan renders): “Had you not interrogated my wife.”



**19**	**a spirit of the Lord.** A spirit of might emanating from God (Targum Jonathan).

**19**	**clothing.** I. e., the clothing that he removed (שֶחָלַץ has similar construction) from them.



**20**	**לְמֵרֵעֵהוּ אֲשֶׁר רֵעָה לוֹ.** She was given to one of his companions.
