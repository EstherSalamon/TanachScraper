**1**	**my son, my son.** My son, my son eight times; seven that brought him forth from the seven levels of Gehinnom, and one that brought him into the world to come.



**5**	**covered his face.** wrapped around his face in the manner of mourners.

**5**	**Heb..** לאט Similar to “and he wrapped (וילט) his face in his mantle” (I Kings 19:13) (and) “It is here wrapped (לוטה) in a cloth” (I Sam. 21:10).



**8**	**If you do not go forth.** Heb. כי אינך, if you do not go forth.



**9**	**but Israel fled.** Those that were with Absalom.



**10**	**argued with one another.** Heb. כל העם נדון, argued with one another.



**12**	**while the talk of all Israel…(to return him) to his house?.** All this is still part of the charge [to Zadok and Abiathar].



**14**	**And to Amasa.** The general [of Absalom’s army] you shall say: “Behold you are my sister’s son.”



**18**	**Split the Jordan.** They split it with their shields.



**19**	**and the barge crossed over.** The ferry with which one crosses the width of the river.



**21**	**The first of all the House of Joseph.** All of Israel have sinned against you and I [have sinned] more than all of them. And behold I have come to beg forgiveness. If you accept me, all of Israel will be assured that you will accept them. And If not they will fear to return to you again.



**23**	**be put to death today.** In interrogative form.

**23**	**That today I am king.** Heretofore I thought that surely such a great man would not abuse me unless my rule had been terminated by God who had commanded him, ‘Curse David.’ Now, however, that he repents, I know that I am king.



**25**	**had not washed his feet.** Heb. עשה, an expression of ‘setting in order’ [in this instance] the removal of hair of the legs.

**25**	**mustache.** Heb. שפמו, Grenon, in French.



**26**	**why had you not gone with me.** Gone forth from Jerusalem when I departed.



**27**	**is lame.** and I cannot go by foot.



**32**	**to escort him..** Heb. לשלחו, to escort him.



**33**	**a very great man.** in wealth.



**35**	**how many are the days of my life.** That I have yet to live.

**35**	**that I should go up.** They are surely few, so what pleasure could I derive from your sustaining me.



**36**	**discern between good and bad? or can I… taste what I eat.** between good food and bad food. Now our Rabbis have stated that he was highly promiscuous and therefore aged rapidly.



**37**	**a short way shall your servant go past.** A short distance shall your servant pass over to escort you, once you cross the Jordan.

**37**	**with this reward.** Which is not [really] good for me.



**38**	**your servant Chimham.** He was his [Barzillai’s] son.



**43**	**Because the king is near to us.** He is from my tribe.

**43**	**has any present.** Heb. נשאת, similar to משאת (Gen. 43:34); a reward from the house of the king.



**44**	**we have ten parts in the king.** for we are ten tribes.

**44**	**to David.** Although he is related to you, I am more drawn to him since I represent ten parts.

**44**	**why then did you slight me?.** By preceding me [in the king’s escort]. Had I not spoken first concerning the return of my king? For [indeed] our words to reinstate him came first; as it is written: “And the word of all of Israel came to the king.”

**44**	**And the words of the men of Judah were harsher.** A similar expression is: “Correct yourselves etc” (Zeph. 2:1). The words of the people of Judah seemed [more] plausible and correct for they showed them a letter that David had sent them [stating]: “Why should you be last to reinstate the king, etc.” (above v. 12). In this manner it is explained in the Aggadah. Now one may also explain ויקש as an expression of harshness and strength. And so did Jonathan translate: ‘were harsher.’
