**1**	**to the border of Edom.** Adjacent to the border of Edom.

**1**	**southward.** at the southern boundary of Eretz Israel.

**1**	**at the uttermost part of the south.** at the end of the entire boundary.



**2**	**And their south border was.** i.e., the southern border of Judah.

**2**	**from the edge of the Salt Sea.** that is the southeastern corner of Eretz Israel, in אֵלֶּה מַסְעֵי.

**2**	**the bay.** [lit., the tongue, i.e., the tongue] of the sea.



**3**	**And it went out to the south side of Maaleh-Acrabbim [Scorpion Pass].** The Scripture uses the terms: וְיָצָא and it went out, וְתָאַר, and it circled וְנָסַב, and it circled, only where the line of the boundary bulges outward, or is indented, and does not follow a straight course. Here, it bulges outward, and comes מִנֶּגֶב לְמַעֲלֵה עַקְרַבּים to the south of Maaleh-Acrabim. Hence, Maaleh-Acrabbim is within the line, and it passed it and continued westward.

**3**	**to Zin.** Heb. צִנָּה, same as לְצִין, Every word which needs a ‘lamed’ prefixed to it, may instead have a ‘he’ suffixed to it.

**3**	**and ascended.** As long as it ascends from the east toward Jerusalem, it ascends, and from Jerusalem and further, it descends. Hence, we learn that Jerusalem is the highest point in all Eretz Israel. Jerusalem is not mentioned in this boundary, for it was in the northern border of Judah, as is mentioned in this chapter.

**3**	**and ascended on the south side of Kadesh-Barnea.** The line goes to the south of Kadesh-Barnea. Hence, Kadesh is within the line.

**3**	**and it passed along to Hezron.** He continues to count westward until: and its goings out (sic) were at the sea.



**4**	**the goings out.** the end of the border is at the Great [Mediterranean] Sea which is the western border of all Eretz Israel. Hence, the boundary of Judah occupies the entire length of Eretz Israel from east to west, and is situated in the south of Eretz Israel.



**5**	**and the east border.** the line which is drawn along the east border of Judah.

**5**	**the Salt Sea.** which is the southeastern corner of the land, in “Eileh Massei.”

**5**	**even to the end of the Jordan.** The breadth of the boundary of Judah is only opposite the Dead Sea, up to the place where the Jordan empties into the Dead Sea, for the Jordan too is in the eastern border of the land of Canaan, as it is stated in “Eileh Mass’ei,” concerning the eastern border; and the border descended to the Jordan, and its goings out were the Salt Sea, which is at the corner.

**5**	**And their border in the north quarter.** The borderline which is drawn along the northern border from east to west, is from the gulf of the Dead Sea, from the place where the Jordan empties into it, as we have stated that the eastern border ends there; and it went up westward to Beth Hoglah, and the line passed on the north of Beth-Arabah in the south of Eretz Israel. Beth-Arabah (sic) hence, Beth Arabah is in the boundary of Judah, within the line.



**6**	**and the border went up to Eben of Bohan.** As long as the border goes toward Jerusalem, it goes up.



**7**	**And the border went up toward Debir from the Valley of Achor.** for the Valley of Achor was between Eben of Bohan and Debir.

**7**	**and so northward, facing Gilgal.** And when the border reached opposite Gilgal, the border widens and the borderline goes out toward the north to Gilgal which is opposite the ascent to Adummim, the ascent being south of the valley. Hence, the valley is outside the line, not in the boundary of Judah.

**7**	**on the south side of the valley.** This was a steep canyon without water.

**7**	**at 'En-rogel.** the fountain of the launderers, according to Jonathan who renders: קַצְרָה עֵין. The scripture calls the launderer רוֹגֵל, because he treads woolen clothing with his feet (רֶגֶל).



**8**	**And the border went up by the valley of the son of Hinnom.** It still goes up a little until 'En-’etam, and from there on it goes down. Now, this is what our Rabbis said in Zeb. : “They thought to build it [the Temple] in 'En-’etam, which is slightly higher than Jerusalem.”

**8**	**to the south side of the Jebusite: the same is Jerusalem.** Jerusalem is found to be outside the line, and not in the boundary of Judah, but in the boundary of Benjamin, which is north of Judah.



**9**	**And circled.** Heb. וְתָאַר an expression similar to וְנָסַב, in a circle, like: “And with a compass he encircles it.” יְתָאֳרֵהוּ Jonathan rendered them all וְיִסְחַר, and he encircled.

**9**	**and it went out to, etc..** The line goes out to the north, and the boundary widens to the cities of Mount Ephron.

**9**	**and the border circled to Baalah.** This is not to the west, but the bulge was to the north.



**10**	**And the border compassed from Baalah westward.** Now he returns to his previous enumeration [of border cities] from east to west.

**10**	**to the side of Mount Jearim, etc..** for the borderline was north of Mt. Jearim. Hence, Mt. Jearim is within the boundary of Judah, within the borderline.



**11**	**and the goings out of the border.** the end of the boundary.

**11**	**at the sea.** at the Great [Med.] Sea which is the western boundary.



**13**	**according to the commandment of the Lord to Joshua.** as the Most Holy, Blessed be He, said to Joshua.



**14**	**And Caleb drove out of there.** after Joshua’s death, for during Joshua’s lifetime Hebron was not yet taken, as it is stated in Jud. It was only written here because of its relation to the division [of the land].



**15**	**Kirjath-sepher.** Our Rabbis said: These are the [3,000] laws which were forgotten during the mourning period for Moses, which Othniel the son of Kenaz reestablished through his great scholarship.



**17**	**the brother of Caleb.** from his mother.



**18**	**and she leaned.** Heb. וְאִתְרְכִינַת (Targum) She bent herself to fall at his feet.



**19**	**sustenance.** Heb. בְרָכָה.

**19**	**an arid land.** Heb. נֶגֶב, like: “the face of the ground was dried,” which Onkelos renders: נגיבו. Sec in French. I.e., a house which is arid [devoid] of all earthly good, a man who has nothing but Torah.

**19**	**you have given me.** Heb. נתתני, i.e., you have given to me [but the preposition is lacking in Hebrew]. Like: “To speak to him (דַּבְּרוֹ) peacefully;” “my children have gone forth from me (יְצָאֻנִי);” “he cooked for them (וּבִשְּׁלָם) the meat.”

**21**	**And the uttermost cities of the tribe of the children of Judah.** on the border of the tribe of Judah. That is what our Rabbis said: Joshua only enumerated the towns on the borders [of every tribe].



**32**	**all the cities are twenty-nine.** When you count them, you find thirty-eight. These extra were taken by the children of Simeon from the territory of the children of Judah, and they are mentioned in the inheritance of Simeon: Beer-sheba, and Moladah, and Hazar-shual, and Ezem, and Eltolad, and Hormah, and Ziklag, and Ain, and Rimmon. These are the extra nine enumerated here.



**36**	**fourteen cities.** When you count them, you find fifteen. I say that Tappuah and Enam are one, and this is its explanation: Tappuah and its spring. That is 'En-tappuah which is mentioned in reference to the inheritance of Manasseh: 'to the inhabitants of ‘En-tappuah.’



**47**	**and its border.** the islands in the sea [adjacent to the coast], isles in old French.



**63**	**the children of Judah could not drive them out.** We learned in Sifrei : Rabbi Joshua the son of Korha says: They really could, but they were not permitted, because of the oath which Abraham had sworn to Abimelech. Now these Jebusites were not of the Jebusite nation, but the Tower of David which was in Jerusalem, was called Jebus, and the inhabitants of that section were of the Philistines. And when the children of Judah conquered Jerusalem, they did not drive out the inhabitants of that section.
