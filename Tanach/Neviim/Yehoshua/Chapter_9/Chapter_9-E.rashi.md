**4**	**And they also acted with wile.** Just as Jacob’s sons dealt cunningly with Hamor the father of Shechem who was a Hivite; and the inhabitants of Gibeon were also from the Hivites, as it is stated in this episode.

**4**	**disguised as ambassadors.** They made themselves like message carriers, from the expression of: “(וְצִיר) And a messenger was sent among the nations.” Now, every word, the beginning of whose root is ‘zade,’ when it is used as reflexive in the form of מתפעל or נתפעל, a ‘teth’ comes in the middle thereof, and separates the root letters of the word, like: מַה נִּצְטַדָּק ‘How will we justify ourselves?’ from the root צדק, [that he says נִצְטַדָּק,] and so: גשמה יִצְטַבַּע ‘his body will be bathed,’ from צבע.

**4**	**wine bottles, rotten.** so that they should appear like people coming from a distant land.

**4**	**and tied up.** a synonym for מְבַקְּעִים, split., This is an Aramaic expression, as דחיטי ציריה, ‘the splits of the wheat kernels,’ דמיצרי זיקי, ‘which split the sacks.’



**5**	**and all the bread… was dry and mouldy.** arsin in French, an expression of מוּקָד, burned [or toasted], and so did Jonathan render כִסְנִין.



**7**	**Perhaps you dwell in my midst.** Perhaps you are from the inhabitants of the land?



**12**	**we took… for our provision.** Heb. הִצְטַיַּדְנוּ, an expression of צֵידָה provisions, i.e., when we took it out for provisions for the journey.



**14**	**And the men took of their provision.** They accepted their words for they captured them with their mouths [מִצֵּידָם meaning from their hunting or capturing] an expression of: “and he who did not (צֵידָה) hunt.”
