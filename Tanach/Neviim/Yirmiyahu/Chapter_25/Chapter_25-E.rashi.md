**1**	**in the fourth year.** The year that their decree was sealed that they be exiled and that they drink the cup of wrath, as he says in this section (v. 15): “Take the cup, etc.,” and before the decree He commanded the prophet to reprove them, perhaps they would repent, and their decree would not be sealed.



**3**	**twenty-three years.** Josiah reigned thirty-one years (II Kings 22:1). Subtract twelve years from them (Jer. 1:2). This leaves nineteen, and four of his son amount to twenty-three.

**3**	**arising early.** This is a present tense. I would arise early and speak constantly.



**10**	**the sound of a mill.** That is an indication of a circumcision feast, since they would grind and crush ingredients for healing the wound.

**10**	**the light of a candle.** An indication of a feast. So we learned in Sanhedrin (32b).



**13**	**all that is written.** concerning Babylon.

**13**	**in this Book.** at its end.



**14**	**For… will enslave them.** Like ‘For… shall enslave them,’ i.e., shall enslave the people of Babylon.

**14**	**many nations… too.** Viz. Media and Persia.



**15**	**cup of the wine of fury.** The prophecy of retribution

**15**	**and you shall give… to drink it.** Prophesy to them that it will no longer be retracted for any repentance.



**16**	**and reel to and fro.** Jonathan renders: and they shall be confused.

**16**	**and be like madmen.** an expression of madness, they shall be mad.



**18**	**as of this day.** As they are today. Jeremiah wrote his Book after the destruction.



**20**	**And all the allies.** All the supports (סוּמְכְוָתָא) which are Pharaoh’s guarantee, and he leans upon them for aid.

**20**	**the allies.** Garantie in French.



**22**	**and the kings of the isle.** All these that he counts (ֹשֶמוֹנֶה) until here are neighbors of Eretz Israel.



**23**	**and all those separated in that corner.** those separated at the end of that corner.



**24**	**the kings of Arabia.** Heb. עֲרָב, Arabia.

**24**	**and all the kings of the allies.** The allies of the Arabs.



**26**	**Sheshach.** That is ‘Babylon’ in אתבש.



**27**	**and vomit.** Like a person who drinks too much, who vomits.

**27**	**the sword.** the strife of war.



**29**	**commence.** מֵחֵל.



**30**	**over His habitation.** He mourns over the destruction of His house.

**30**	**“Hedad!”.** A cry of “Ha!” that those engaged in hard work cry out aloud to urge each other on to strengthen themselves, and those who tread the winepresses are accustomed to it.

**30**	**He shall call out.** Every expression of עֲנִיָה, is an expression of a cry with a loud voice.



**31**	**He contends.** He contends in judgment.



**32**	**from nation to nation.** whom I incite against one another.

**32**	**from the ends.** as the Targum renders it: מִסְיָפֵי, from the ends of.



**34**	**Howl.** an expression of יְלָלָה, howling.

**34**	**and roll.** i.e., roll in the dust (se vautrer in French).

**34**	**and your scatterings.** This is a short verse; i.e., to say: and your scatterings shall come near.

**34**	**like a precious vessel.** That is to say: If you say, “We are important,” there are many precious glass vessels that fall, break, and are lost.



**37**	**shall be destroyed.** an expression of destruction, an expression related to ‘has silenced us (הֲדִמָּנוּ).’ ‘and there let us sit in silence (וְנִדְּמָה).’



**38**	**He has left his hut like a young lion.** The king has gone in exile from his city like a young lion that leaves his hut.

**38**	**because of the wrath of the oppressing sword.** drunk from wine. So did Jonathan translate. Another explanation: It is an expression of oppression (אוֹנָאָה).
