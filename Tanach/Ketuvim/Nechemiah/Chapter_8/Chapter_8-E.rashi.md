**2**	**on the first day of the seventh month.** That is the day of Rosh Hashanah.



**3**	**from the light.** of the beginning of the day.

**3**	**to the Scroll of the Law.** All the people inclined their ears.



**4**	**on a wooden tower.** That wooden tower was made for the purpose of reading the Scroll of the Law upon it.



**5**	**for he was above all the people.** Therefore, they all saw him.

**5**	**and when he opened it, all the people stood.** And when he opened it to read, all the people stood silent.

**5**	**stood.** Heb. עָמְדוּ, an expression of silence (they stood silent), like (Job 32:16): “… for they remained silent (עָמְדוּ); they no longer answered.”



**6**	**with the uplifting of their hands.** with the raising of their hands, meaning that they lifted their hands on high to thank the Holy One, blessed be He, as it is stated: (I Kings 8:22): “… and spread forth his hands towards heaven.”



**7**	**explained… to the people.** meaning that they would translate the words of the Torah to the people.

**7**	**in their place.** meaning that they were standing on their feet.



**8**	**and gave sense.** Heb. וְשּׂם שֶׂכֶל, and giving wisdom; וְשּׂם is an expression of doing.



**9**	**The day is holy.** because it is the day of Rosh Hashanah.

**9**	**for all the people were weeping.** because they did not uphold the Torah appropriately.



**10**	**to whoever has nothing prepared.** to a poor man who has no food prepared for himself.



**11**	**quieted.** They admonished all the people to be quiet, that they should weep no longer.

**11**	**Hush.** Heb. הַסוּ, an expression of silence, like (Num. 13:30): “And Caleb hushed (וַיַהַס).”



**13**	**And on the second day.** of Rosh Hashanah.



**15**	**And that they should announce.** And they commanded that they announce that they celebrate the Festival of Sukkoth, and so it is customary for Scripture to speak in this manner, like (I Sam. 9:27): “Tell the servant and he will go ahead of us.”

**15**	**myrtle leaves.** It is explained in Tractate Succah (12a) that this is a wild myrtle which is unfit for the lulav and only fit to make a sukkah.

**15**	**date palm leaves.** for a lulav.

**15**	**and leaves of plaited trees.** This is the myrtle that is fit for the lulav as is explained in Tractate Succah (ad loc.).



**18**	**day by day.** every day.
